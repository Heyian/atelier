# Models and effort

Claude doesn't come in one size. It comes in families you can think of as
tiers, sorted by shape rather than by version: a **fast** tier built for
quick, lightweight answers; a **balanced** tier that sits in the middle;
and a **deep** tier built to work through something hard and give it real
thought before answering. Which tier you pick changes how Claude approaches
your question, not just how long you wait for the reply.

Separately from the tier, there's **effort** — a setting for how much
thinking Claude does before it answers at all. Higher effort means Claude
spends more time weighing angles, checking its own reasoning, and working
through the problem before committing to a reply. Lower effort means a
faster, more direct answer, with less of that deliberation behind it.

Both settings trade speed for depth, and that trade-off is the whole point:
pick the deepest tier and the highest effort for a decision you're actually
going to act on — something with real consequences if it's wrong. Pick the
fastest tier and the lowest effort for a throwaway rewrite, a quick draft
you'll glance at once and move past. Matching the setting to the stakes,
rather than defaulting to the deepest tier for everything, is the skill to
build here.

Somewhere in the interface — usually in the conversation's own settings, or
right near where you type your message — you'll find the controls for
both: one to choose which model answers, another for how much effort it
puts in.

## Try it on your own

Open the conversation settings, near where you type your message, and look
at what you have. Take the most important conversation of your week — the
one you're actually going to act on — and run it again once at the highest
effort level. Compare the two answers.
